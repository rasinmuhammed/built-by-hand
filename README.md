## Why this exists

I shipped production data and LLM systems (Databricks pipelines, entity resolution, RAG) with heavy AI assistance. The output was real. The foundations underneath it were thinner than the output suggested, and I knew it before anyone asked me to prove it.

So I stopped, and gave myself six months to fix it properly: build things from first principles, write my own core logic, publish benchmarks with real numbers, and record myself explaining each system without notes.

This repo is the trail. It is deliberately unflattering in places, because the failures are logged alongside the wins. A log that only contains wins is evidence of nothing.

**Where I am heading:** AI Engineer who makes LLM systems work on messy real-world data (RAG, entity resolution, synthetic data, and the pipelines underneath) and who can sit with a customer and ship.

---

## The rules I am holding myself to

These are the point. Anyone can commit code. These constrain *how* it gets written.

1. **Write-first.** For any core logic, my full attempt exists before AI sees it. AI reviews, explains, and scaffolds boilerplate. It does not draft the thing I am claiming to understand.
2. **Explain-to-commit.** Nothing gets committed that I cannot explain line by line, out loud.
3. **Docs, then Stack Overflow, then AI.** In that order.
4. **Numbers or it did not happen.** Every project carries latency, cost, and accuracy against a named baseline.
5. **Ship weekly.** A week with no public artifact is a failed week, regardless of hours worked.
6. **The monthly cold check.** Last Friday of each month I open code I wrote three weeks earlier, close every other tab, and explain it to a recording with no notes. Whatever I cannot explain, I re-derive that weekend.

I would rather be measured against these than against a plan. Plans are easy to publish and easy to miss quietly. Rules are visible in every commit.
